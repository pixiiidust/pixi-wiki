02 · Starting point

How Pham came to AI

Pham

I started using AI about a week before Fable 5 was released. I came to it late, but I wanted to understand what was possible, so I began studying and obsessing over how AI behaves and what it is.

I hadn’t read the existing AI literature. I followed my own pathways and independently arrived at ideas that seemed to point toward the same unifying principles.

03 · The work

Working at the boundary

Pham

I viewed the problem through epistemology, consilience, and provenance: how we know something, how different kinds of evidence support one another, and where each claim or result came from.

I wanted to understand the edge of programs that could not be tested in the usual way—problems with no known solution, but where an answer could still be bounded and gradually approached.

I worked to build up an understanding of computational physics and mathematics, then tried to make a multi-front, concurrent move across all of known physics.

Pham shared supporting work alongside this message, including Tapestry PDFs, a computational physics portfolio, the Huygens Chronicle, Proof Machine briefs, an almanac, and a campaign sheet. The file sizes and preview fragments have been removed here.

06 · Bounded unknowns

Looking into unknown problems

Pham

The sense I have is that many problems are unknown, but we know their edges and can bound them. We can look at different boundaries, versions, and formulations of the same problem.

We cannot see into the shadow at first. We send one ray of light into it, then another from a different angle. When the rays combine, we begin to see.

07 · Knowing

How can we know what is true?

Pham

I asked myself: how can anyone know anything? How can we know whether an AI is lying or telling the truth? How can we know whether anything a human says is true?

For me, this is tied to the unity of knowledge and its resonance. Everything must unite as one around a common center—the underlying truth we all share. This is Truth. This is God. It is the underlying source from which everything else must derive.

Everything must cascade from what we know at the beginning. Evidence from every angle must tie together as one.

08 · Convergence

Many agents, one conclusion

Pham

We send agents out from many different angles, then bring them back together. They must converge on the same conclusion.

A single AI agent is fallible. But when many agents move outward independently and return to the same truth, they begin to resonate with the same song—a singular song that can carry into every corner.

09 · The limit

Where Pham felt stuck

Pham

I still felt so stuck.

I had mapped out known computational physics: fluid dynamics, electromagnetics, mechanics, and chemical evolution.

But I was stuck. They united and sang as one, but they were still in the domain of the trivial.
