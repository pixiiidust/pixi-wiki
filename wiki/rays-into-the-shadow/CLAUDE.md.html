Rays into the Shadow Namespace Instructions¶

This namespace is an open, annotated conversation. It is not a finished essay.

On this page
  • Source rules
  • Conversation rules
  • Chapter rules
  • Plain-language rules
  • Evidence rules

Source rules¶

  • Add each new conversation segment once under raw/transcripts/YYYY-MM-DD-<slug>.md.
  • Treat earlier raw files as immutable.
  • Preserve the words, names, dates, and uncertainty in quoted source text.
  • Do not silently correct a speaker inside a quotation.
  • Do not infer the contents of an attachment from its filename, title, or size.

Conversation rules¶

  • Treat Jamie (Pixiedust) and Pham as the two main speakers. The introducer opens the exchange.
  • Use their turns as the north star. Organize downstream concepts by the question, claim, or reply that introduced them.
  • Ask what each reply changes. Do not rank a speaker's importance by message length.
  • Do not present Pham's ideas as a free-standing system with Jamie at the edge.
  • Do not invent a missing reply. Mark the point where the supplied source stops.
  • Define ontology as “what exists in the conversation” and epistemology as “how a claim may be known or checked” before using those terms.

Chapter rules¶

  • Add chapters under wiki/syntheses/chapters/ with zero-padded numbers.
  • Keep one main theme per chapter.
  • Use a source passage, then a short editorial note, then any open questions.
  • Add new chapters instead of forcing later themes into old chapters.
  • Update wiki/index.md and append to wiki/log.md on every content change.
  • Keep the status ongoing until the speakers say the conversation is complete.

Plain-language rules¶

Use George Orwell's “Politics and the English Language” as an editing test for editorial prose:

  • prefer familiar, concrete words;
  • use a short word when it says the same thing;
  • cut words that add no meaning;
  • prefer active voice;
  • avoid stale figures of speech and decorative jargon;
  • use a technical term only when it is the exact term needed;
  • break a style rule rather than make the sentence ugly or false.

Ask of each sentence: What does it claim? Who does the action? What evidence supports it? What does it leave open?

Evidence rules¶

  • Label speaker claims as speaker claims.
  • Treat agreement among agents as evidence, not proof.
  • Check whether apparently different routes share a source, model, prompt, or assumption.
  • Keep scientific, philosophical, and theological claims distinct even when the conversation relates them.
  • Say “not reviewed” when the source body is unavailable.

Plain static HTML. Humans browse it like a wiki; agents read Markdown through llms.txt.
