## Editor's note

The central point is narrow: some problems have no known answer that can serve as a full test. That does not make every check impossible.

A researcher may still test a bound, a special case, a conservation law, a known limit, or agreement with another method. These checks do not reveal the whole answer. They mark the edges of what an answer must satisfy.

The source uses three technical words:

- **Epistemology:** how we know.
- **Consilience:** support from different kinds of evidence.
- **Provenance:** where a claim, datum, or result came from.

These words stay because each names a distinct part of the problem. The rest can be said plainly: approach the unknown from several directions, keep the path, and compare the results.
