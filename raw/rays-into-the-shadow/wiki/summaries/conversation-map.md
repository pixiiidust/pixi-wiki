# Conversation Map — The Exchange Comes First

This book begins with an exchange between **Jamie, writing as Pixiedust, and Pham**. The ideas downstream must stay tied to their turns.

The rule is simple: do not arrange the book as a catalogue of Pham's ideas with Jamie at the edge. Arrange it as a conversation. Ask what one speaker says, how the other replies, and what new question the reply creates.

| 2 | Pham | Describes a recent start with AI, independent work, and a large set of artifacts. | Provenance, independent arrival, and the scope of the work. |

| 7 | Jamie | No reply after these longer remarks appears in the supplied source. | The next source must supply this turn; the editor must not invent it. |

## Ontology — what exists in the conversation

Here, **ontology** means the kinds of things the speakers talk about.

- **Speakers:** Jamie (Pixiedust), Pham, and the introducer.
- **Turns:** dated messages and replies.
- **Questions:** what got stuck; how truth can be known; what lies beyond known results.
- **Claims:** statements a speaker presents as true.
- **Methods:** bounds, independent routes, rays, and many-agent comparison.
- **Artifacts:** files, briefs, links, and portfolios named in the exchange.
- **Boundaries:** testable and non-testable; known and unknown; agreement and proof; established work and discovery.
- **Open questions:** matters the speakers have not settled.

No concept floats free of this list. Each concept page must point back to the turn that introduced it or the reply that changed it.

## Epistemology — how the book handles knowledge

Here, **epistemology** means how a claim may be known or checked.

1. **Attribute the claim.** Say who made it.
2. **Keep the turn.** Show what the claim answered or prompted.
3. **Name the source.** Link the dated transcript or reviewed artifact.
4. **State the status.** Mark it as a question, speaker claim, editorial inference, tested result, or open matter.
5. **Show the limit.** Say what the evidence does not establish.
6. **Keep disagreement.** Do not smooth two views into one voice.

Agreement among agents may support a claim. It does not prove the claim. An attachment may support a claim after someone reads and checks it. A filename alone does not.

## Maintenance test

Before adding a page, answer four questions:

- Which turn gives rise to it?
- Which speaker's words or question does it preserve?
- What is source, and what is editorial inference?
- What later reply could revise it?

If those answers are missing, the page does not yet belong in this book.
